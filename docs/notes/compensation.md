## What the rules say (re-verify before changing copy; the page prints the date it was read)
- 25% of the price paid for 60-119 min, 50% from 120, measured at the destination on the ticket (doors open) against the published timetable. Return ticket sold at one price: half the fare. DB pays nothing under EUR 4. Same percentages for Sparpreis/Super Sparpreis/Flexpreis.
- No compensation if the rider took the Art. 18 refund, was rerouted and arrived under 60 min late, or the delay was announced before purchase.
- Refusal (Art. 19(10), since June 2023): extreme weather/disasters, public health crisis, passenger fault, unavoidable third-party acts (persons on track, cable theft, police, on-board emergency, sabotage). NOT own-staff strikes, other operators on the track, or infrastructure/station-manager failures. DB says ordinary storms do not count and it pays after strikes. Track works, signal faults and train defects are not on the list (inferred, no case law found).
- Deadline conflict: Regulation says complaints within 3 months; DB currently accepts ~12 months "until further notice"; its conditions time-bar claims one year after ticket validity. Publish both and say "claim soon".
- Season tickets: EUR 5/7.50 per 60+ min case (delays of 20+ min may be added, cap 25%); BahnCard 100 EUR 10/15, cap 25%, no adding. Deutschland-Ticket is not valid on ICE/IC/EC (listed exceptions); a regional+ICE combination is two contracts.
- Claim channels: DB Navigator, bahn.de account/order search, form by post to Servicecenter Fahrgastrechte (60647 Frankfurt), EU form; then Schlichtungsstelle Reise & Verkehr (free), Eisenbahn-Bundesamt.
- Self-booking (Art. 18(3)): needs an expected delay of 60+ min; the 100 minutes run from the scheduled departure of the delayed or cancelled train OR of the missed connection (not of the trip start); cost must be necessary and reasonable; DB does not pay taxi, plane or private pick-up in that case (it does pay up to EUR 120 for other transport at night).
- Assistance (Art. 20): meals, hotel, transport off a stuck train from 60 min delay. The 3-night hotel cap applies when the delay has an Art. 19(10) cause, not whenever DB refuses; assistance is owed even then.
- Delay is measured against the arrival time of the journey booked, at the station on the ticket, even if the rider took another train or route. Never word it as "the planned arrival time" right after "another train": it reads as the replacement train's.
- Through ticket (Art. 12): tickets bought together in one booking from a railway are ONE through ticket unless the rider was told before buying that they are separate contracts. Only truly separate tickets give no right for a missed connection, just DB's goodwill scheme (international trips, enough change time needed).
- Complaint to the Eisenbahn-Bundesamt (Art. 33(1)): within 3 months of the first refusal, or if there is no reply 3 months after the claim. DB must pay within one month of a claim (Art. 19(7)); money instead of a voucher on request.
- Pending: an EU enforcement package (rail: duty to name the specific cause when refusing) passed Parliament, Council approval open; a proposal on multi-operator tickets is not law.

## Framing
- The site measures delay at the train's LAST stop; a claim follows the ticket (destination, price, cancellations, missed connections). The figures are an indicator, never "you are owed"; the page says so under the tiles.
- Do not write refund claims beyond the verified rules file; anything uncertain gets a caveat or is cut.
- Wording that passed review: tile subtitles "of trains, at their last stop"; basis note "... long-distance train runs that reached their last stop this late ... Cancelled trains are not counted."; guide note "These figures count trains, not riders ... they show how often trains ran this late, not how often riders were owed money." The "60+" tile also contains the 120+ trains, so it is not the share paying 25%. The routes subtitle and month copy use the same trains-at-last-stop wording. State the disclaimer "we are not the Deutsche Bahn and cannot claim for you" with the full name.
- Meta description for the page stays near 155-160 characters; the first draft (~230) was cut.
- Old version passed three review rounds but the owner called it "such a mess": long rule blocks on a ~6,200 px phone page.

## Plain-language redesign ("a page for the normies")
Owner brief: DB makes this bureaucratic; explain with diagrams and visuals, not walls of text. Structure that was built (`CompensationPage.tsx`, `Compensation.module.css`, client `CompCalc.tsx`, strings `comp2.*` next to the old `comp.*`):
1. Title + one-line scope, then the rule as ONE ruler: three zones 0% / 25% / 50% at 0-60-120 min (grey track / yellow / orange, the only colours on the page, reused everywhere).
2. Calculator: price paid + slider over the same zoned ruler + 'return ticket at one price' (half fare) -> euro amount; under 60 min and under EUR 4 show their own sentence.
3. 'What counts': a trip drawn as start / change / destination dots + 7 yes/no rows (destination on the ticket, missed connection on one ticket/booking, cancelled train, cheap tickets; no: delay known at purchase, refund taken, < EUR 4).
4. 'Running late right now?': ladder 20 / 60 / 100 min (train binding lifted; refund or reroute or keep going + assistance; self-book after 100 min).
5. 'Can DB say no?': two columns DB pays / DB can refuse, one fine note (assistance still owed, hotel cap 3 nights, 'our reading' for works/faults).
6. 'Claim in 3 steps' + one black CTA to bahn.de + a dashed 'DB said no?' box.
7. 'How often': '1 in N' big + 100-dot grid (60-119 vs 120+) + the trains-not-riders note.
8. Folds: routes, months, season tickets, 'The fine print' (the reviewed old `comp.*` sentences, kept verbatim), then sources + checked date.
